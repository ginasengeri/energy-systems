PJM Market Resdesign

PJM's Regulation Market underwent a redesign in October 2025, with the implementation of a unified regulation signal. The previous design divided participation between technologies with two separate signals: Traditional slow-ramping resources followed a low-pass filtered RegA signal, and faster-responding resources, majorly batteries, followed the high-pass RegD signal. Beginning October 1st, the two signals were merged into one bidirectional signal, allowing all technologies to compete and respond uniformly to grid balancing needs.

Brief clarification of what improvements/achievements were made by the redesign thus far.

Despite an insignificant difference in LMPs across the two quarters, co-optimized regulation prices more than doubled in Q4, pointing to a cause independent of market stability/lack of volatility. Some of this is explainable by the increase in accessible market share for batteries. As participation of batteries in the RM grows, these prices are expected to come down.

The model explores revenue earnings before and after the redesign. Different contract structures are modeled for Q4 to show the results of varying degrees of market exposure and the risks involved.

Model Formulation

The model estimates gross revenue for a 100MW/400MWh battery operating in PJM, AEP-Dayton trading hub. The formulation is based on the BESS model developed by Alharbi, Alsaidan and Gao, with the following adaptations made for compatibility with available data:

  1. AGC signal. The regulation signal is constructed from ACE. For the pre-redesign period, a filter is applied to ACE before normalization, to approximate the faster RegD signal [link]. For the post-redesign period, ACE is normalized without this filtering, producing a slower signal than RegD but faster than RegA [link].
  2. Post-redesign settlement. RMPCP is replaced by RMMCP in the post-redesign revenue calculation, as per PJM Manual [number] updates [link].
  3. Time resolution. Publically available market and signal data are hourly. The sub-hourly interval index used in the reference model is replaced with an hourly basis instead.

Objective

The model maximizes gross revenue from energy arbitrage and regulation:

\[ \max R = \sum_{t\in T} \left( R^{arb}_t + R^{reg,cap}_t + R^{reg,mil}_t \right) \tag{1} \]

where \(R^{arb}_t\) is the product of net arbitrage and the AEP-Dayton LMP, \(R^{reg, cap}_t\) is the product of the RMCCP, regulation capacity committed and performance factor, and \(R^{reg,mil}_t\) is the product of RMMCP, regulation capacity moved (commited*mileage), and the performance factor.

Key Constraints

The model represents three aspects of battery operation: (1) the allocation of power between arbitrage and regulation, (2) the physical response to the regulation signal and its resulting SOC, and (3) a limit on battery cycling.

1. Battery dispatch

Energy arbitrage and regulation compete for the battery's available power capability; the dispatch is bounded by the 100 MW power rating. In addition, the battery can not simultaneously charge and discharge:

\[ 0 \leq P^{arb,d}_t \leq P^{max}u^d_t, \qquad -P^{max}u^c_t \leq P^{arb,c}_t \leq 0 \tag{2} \]

Binary variables \(u^d_t\) and \(u^d_t\) take values 0 and 1, preventing simultaneous charge and discharge. Regulation capacity is similarly limited by the remaining battery headroom, accounting for the arbitrage position and regulation state.

Products of binary and continuous variables are required throughout this model. To preserve the MILP structure, Fortet and McCormick linearizations are applied to these products.

2. Regulation signal, actual response and SOC

The regulation bid represents capacity made available to the market, while the actual regulation power moved by the battery is determined by the normalized regulation signal. The actual regulation response is represented by:

\[ J_t = J_{t-1} + P^{reg,act,d}_t + P^{reg,act,c}_t \tag{3} \]

where \(J_t\) represents the accumulated regulation movement, carrying forward the regulation movement from the previous interval. In this model's convention, charging is negative and discharging is positive, so a positive \(J_t\) represents a net regulation discharge (RegUp) position, and negative represents a net regulation charge (RegDown) position.

The resulting battery SOC is then determined by both scheduled arbitrage and actual regulation movement:

\[ SOC_t = SOC_{t-1} - \frac{ P^{arb,d}_t + P^{arb,c}_t + J_t }{E^{max}} \Delta t \tag{4} \]

subject to:

\[ SOC^{min} \leq SOC_t \leq SOC^{max}. \tag{5} \]

3. Cycling limit

Battery use is additionally restricted by a maximum cycling limit of 1.5 cycles per day:

\[ C_t \leq C^{max}. \tag{6} \]
