PJM Market Resdesign

PJM's Regulation Market underwent Phase 1 of a redesign in October 2025, implementing a unified bidirectional regulation signal. The previous design procured regulation using two separate signals: a low-pass filtered RegA signal followed by slow-ramping resources, and a high-pass RegD signal followed by faster-responding resources—majorly batteries. Beginning October 1st, the two signals were merged into one, allowing all technologies to compete and respond uniformly to grid balancing needs.

The redesign was followed by a significant increase in regulation prices. Despite relatively stable LMPs across the two quarters, co-optimized regulation prices more than doubled post-redesign, increasing revenue opportunities for batteries. A merchant energy storage system (ESS) earned $ 23.00 kW-month in Q3 and $ 58.14 kW-month in Q4 (monthly average), post-redesign.

A model of a fully merchant battery operating in PJM's AEP-Dayton trading hub illustrates the rise in regulation prices after the redesign.

An ESS Model in PJM

This model estimates gross revenue for a 100MW/400MWh battery using a reference MILP formulation[1]. The following adaptations are made for compatibility with available data:

  1. AGC signal. The regulation signal is constructed from ACE. For the pre-redesign period, a filter is applied to ACE before normalization, to approximate the faster RegD signal [link]. For the post-redesign period, ACE is normalized without this filtering, producing a slower signal than RegD but faster than RegA [link].
  2. Post-redesign settlement. RMPCP is replaced by RMMCP in the post-redesign revenue calculation, as per PJM Manual [number] updates [link].
  3. Time resolution. Publically available market and signal data are hourly. The sub-hourly interval index used in the reference model is replaced with an hourly basis instead.

Objective

Gross revenue from energy arbitrage and regulation is maximized:

\[ \max R = \sum_{t\in T} \left( R^{arb}_t + R^{reg,cap}_t + R^{reg,mil}_t \right) \tag{1} \]

where \(R^{arb}_t\) is the revenue earned from net arbitrage, \(R^{reg, cap}_t\) is the revenue earned from capacity committed into the regulation market, and \(R^{reg,mil}_t\) is revenue earned from actual power moved in the regulation market.

Three contract structures are modelled, and the objective shifts for each:

  1. Toll: the battery is operated and dispatched by a third-party offtaker in exchange for a fixed capacity fee and a variable discharge fee. Risk is allocated fully to the optimizer. The capacity rate is set at $12/kW-month and the variable tolling fee at $9/MWh [https://energy-optima.com/blog/ppa-structuring-solar-storage.htm].
  2. Index PPA: a third party operates the asset and the project owner receives a fixed floor payment in addition to a share of merchant earnings. The model assumes a 40% split share to the asset owner and a base payment of $7/kW-month [https://energy-optima.com/blog/ppa-structuring-solar-storage.htm].
  3. Merchant: Full merchant exposure. The project owner assumes merchant risk and takes earnings directly from the market's conditions.

Key Constraints

Three aspects of battery operation are represented in the model: (1) the allocation of power between arbitrage and regulation, (2) the physical response to the regulation signal and its resulting state of charge (SOC), and (3) a limit on battery cycling:

1. Battery dispatch

Energy arbitrage and regulation compete for the battery's available power capability; dispatch is bounded by the 100 MW power rating. In addition, the battery can not simultaneously charge and discharge:

\[ 0 \leq P^{arb,d}_t \leq P^{max}u^d_t, \qquad -P^{max}u^c_t \leq P^{arb,c}_t \leq 0 \tag{2} \]

Binary variables \(u^d_t\) and \(u^d_t\) take values 0 or 1, preventing simultaneous charge and discharge. Regulation capacity is similarly limited by the remaining battery headroom, accounting for the arbitrage position and regulation state. To preserve the MILP structure, linearizations are applied to binary and continuous variable products.

2. Regulation signal, actual response and SOC

The regulation bid represents capacity made available to the market, while the actual regulation power moved by the battery is determined by the normalized regulation signal. The actual regulation response is represented by:

\[ J_t = J_{t-1} + P^{reg,act,d}_t + P^{reg,act,c}_t \tag{3} \]

where \(J_t\) represents the accumulated regulation movement, carrying forward movement from the previous interval. In this model's convention, charging is negative and discharging is positive, so a positive \(J_t\) represents a net discharge position, and negative represents a net charge position.

The resulting battery SOC is determined by both scheduled arbitrage and actual regulation movement:

\[ SOC_t = SOC_{t-1} - \frac{ P^{arb,d}_t + P^{arb,c}_t + J_t }{E^{max}} \Delta t \tag{4} \]

subject to:

\[ SOC^{min} \leq SOC_t \leq SOC^{max}. \tag{5} \]

3. Cycling limit

The battery is additionally restricted by a maximum cycling limit of 1.5 cycles per day:

\[ C_t \leq C^{max}. \tag{6} \]

Merchant: highest risk, highest reward

Of the contracts modelled, a fully merchant battery had the highest returns in both quarters. The significant increase in upside seen in Q4 was a direct result of the 130% growth in regulation prices.

A fully merchant battery earned x2.53 times more in Q4, post-redesign

The opposite effect can happen at the same magnitude.

Regulation prices are expected to settle closer to pre-redesign levels or lower as participation grows, suppressing the major source of merchant spreads seen in Q4 2025. Retaining the same risk level at full merchant exposure with thinner spreads would leave the battery more vulnerable to unfavourable market conditions.

PJM's market is still in the middle of its redesign, with Phase 2 scheduled for 2027. The MMU has recommended ammendments to the calculation for regulatation clearing prices, prior to implementation of Phase 2, expected to refuce the extremity of the current regulation price spikes. The changing rules require operaters to closely monitor and anticipate how best to position their asset in the market.
