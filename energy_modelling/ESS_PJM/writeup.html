PJM Market Resdesign

PJM's Regulation Market underwent Phase 1 of a redesign in October 2025, implementing a unified bidirectional regulation signal. The previous design procured regulation using two separate signals: a low-pass filtered RegA signal followed by slow-ramping resources, and a high-pass RegD signal followed by faster-responding resources—majorly batteries. Beginning October 1st, the two signals were merged into one, allowing all technologies to compete and respond uniformly to grid balancing needs.

The redesign was followed by a significant increase in regulation prices. Despite relatively stable LMPs across the two quarters, co-optimized regulation prices more than doubled post-redesign, increasing revenue opportunities for batteries.

Phase 2 of the redesign is scheduled for 2027. Changing market rules require battery operaters to closely monitor and anticipate how best to position their asset to capture opportunity in the market. Selecting the right contract structures that balance risk and stable cashflow are key to a financially performing asset.

The model estimates gross revenue for a 100MW/400MWh battery using a reference formulation developed by Alharbi, Alsaidan and Gao [link]. The following adaptations are made for compatibility with available data:

  1. AGC signal. The regulation signal is constructed from ACE. For the pre-redesign period, a filter is applied to ACE before normalization, to approximate the faster RegD signal [link]. For the post-redesign period, ACE is normalized without this filtering, producing a slower signal than RegD but faster than RegA [link].
  2. Post-redesign settlement. RMPCP is replaced by RMMCP in the post-redesign revenue calculation, as per PJM Manual [number] updates [link].
  3. Time resolution. Publically available market and signal data are hourly. The sub-hourly interval index used in the reference model is replaced with an hourly basis instead.

Objective

Gross revenue from energy arbitrage and regulation is maximized:

\[ \max R = \sum_{t\in T} \left( R^{arb}_t + R^{reg,cap}_t + R^{reg,mil}_t \right) \tag{1} \]

where \(R^{arb}_t\) is the product of net arbitrage and the AEP-Dayton LMP, \(R^{reg, cap}_t\) is the product of the RMCCP, regulation capacity committed and performance factor, and \(R^{reg,mil}_t\) is the product of RMMCP, regulation capacity moved (commited*mileage), and the performance factor.

Key Constraints

The model represents three aspects of battery operation: (1) the allocation of power between arbitrage and regulation, (2) the physical response to the regulation signal and its resulting SOC, and (3) a limit on battery cycling.

1. Battery dispatch

Energy arbitrage and regulation compete for the battery's available power capability; the dispatch is bounded by the 100 MW power rating. In addition, the battery can not simultaneously charge and discharge:

\[ 0 \leq P^{arb,d}_t \leq P^{max}u^d_t, \qquad -P^{max}u^c_t \leq P^{arb,c}_t \leq 0 \tag{2} \]

Binary variables \(u^d_t\) and \(u^d_t\) take values 0 or 1, preventing simultaneous charge and discharge. Regulation capacity is similarly limited by the remaining battery headroom, accounting for the arbitrage position and regulation state.

*Model implementation note* Products of binary and continuous variables are required throughout this model. To preserve the MILP structure, Fortet and McCormick linearizations are applied to these products.

2. Regulation signal, actual response and SOC

The regulation bid represents capacity made available to the market, while the actual regulation power moved by the battery is determined by the normalized regulation signal. The actual regulation response is represented by:

\[ J_t = J_{t-1} + P^{reg,act,d}_t + P^{reg,act,c}_t \tag{3} \]

where \(J_t\) represents the accumulated regulation movement, carrying forward the regulation movement from the previous interval. In this model's convention, charging is negative and discharging is positive, so a positive \(J_t\) represents a net regulation discharge, and negative represents a net regulation charge position.

The resulting battery SOC is then determined by both scheduled arbitrage and actual regulation movement:

\[ SOC_t = SOC_{t-1} - \frac{ P^{arb,d}_t + P^{arb,c}_t + J_t }{E^{max}} \Delta t \tag{4} \]

subject to:

\[ SOC^{min} \leq SOC_t \leq SOC^{max}. \tag{5} \]

3. Cycling limit

Battery use is additionally restricted by a maximum cycling limit of 1.5 cycles per day:

\[ C_t \leq C^{max}. \tag{6} \]

Implications

Merchant earned the most in both quarters, but significant increase in upside was seen in Q4, soley due to regulation prices. Energy prices remained relatively stable across both quarters, and regulation capability prices more than doubled. The result: A battery earned $ 23.00 kW-month in Q3 and $ 58.14 kW-month in Q4 (x2.53, monthly average).

The risk: the opposite effect can happen at the same magnitude.

The heightened regulation prices have persisted and grown into 2026, peaking in Feb 2026. Further inspection from the MMU reveal a structural design flaw in regulation price calculations that are inflating prices unfairly. Initially scheduled for October 2026, phase 2 of the redesign has been re-scheduled/delad to 2027, with MMU recommending ammendments to the rmccp calculation (the LOC) to first be implemented before phase 2, aiming to reduce the extremity of the post-phase-1 prices/ The MMU has recommended alterations to the calculation for these clearing prices to remove the falsely inflated and growing rmccps.

If regulation prices fall closer to pre-redesign levels, the majority source of merchant upside would be suppressed, leaving spreads thin while retaining the same risk level of full merchant exposure. Fluctuation of energy prices below the average level would put the asset at greater financial risk with these thinner spreads. It is vital for owners to closely monitor and anticipate how the MMU's proposed changes will impact the returns of their revenue stacking/contract structure/earnings.

Asset owners need to monitor and anticipate how these changes will affect their revenues and determine whether changes will be required in the structure of their revenue stacks.

Bottom line/Outlook/What to look out for

The redesign highlights the market's priority: to maintain a competitive market that efficiently procures the resources it requires, removing mechanisms that favour individual technologies. RegD was designed to be a fast-moving signal that ensures power balance, suited to fast-responding batteries with duration-limit protection. The redesign removed these alterations in design to purely reflect what the market needs and allow available resources to meet those needs.

Resources are rewarded for precise correction of energy imbalances in the market, and individually work around operational limits, while respecting market rules. This effectively clears technologies that operate uncompetitively for a service from the market, allowing room for more efficient technologies to grow. Asset operators are responsible for discovering the combination of services they are most efficient at providing, and discovering their true value in the market.

Changing rules and market conditions (growing participation will saturate regulation over time, energy prices will change market scarcity/volatility levels) to achieve the market's goals will continue to shift profit margins and risk/will affect revenues. The upcoming Phase 2 of the redesign in scheduled for 2027, splitting the bidirectional signal into a RegUp and RegDown parts. Optimizers must closely monitor and anticipate how their route-to-market strategies hold as the market rules change to align with their priorities. They must choose the structure that best suites their asset.
