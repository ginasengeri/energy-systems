System Overview

Germany sits between several different power systems in Europe. It connects a nuclear-heavy system in France, a hydro-dominated system in Norway, and a gas/wind mix in the Netherlands, among many others. The three zones are the focus of this analysis. Flows through Germany show how electricity moves between different types of generation systems, allowing for observation of cross-border trade patterns in one place.

There are three main roles observed in the Germany-Luxembourg zone:

  1. Major importing corridor — France’s nuclear power is the main source of imports.
  2. Alternating flows — gas and wind power from the Netherlands provides balancing support to cover short-term gaps on either side.
  3. Prolonged flow reversals — Norway’s hydro provides seasonal balancing depending on reservoir levels.

Implications

Germany is currently a net importer of electricity, which it first became in 2023, after being a major exporter, due to higher costs of domestic generation (coal) and the shutdown of nuclear power.
