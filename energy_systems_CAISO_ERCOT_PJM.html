The Duck Curve

Increasing renewable buildout has seen a shift in net load profiles, with a drop in demand during the day followed by steep ramping in the evenings. The resulting price spreads provide an opportunity for arbitrage where batteries shift energy from cheap to expensive hours. CAISO, ERCOT and PJM demonstrate this effect with varying degrees of battery penetration.

CAISO demonstrates the strongest duck curve effect, having the largest share of solar relative to its grid size. Abundant solar generation is captured in the middle of the day and then discharged for extended hours to meet peak evening demand. ERCOT's renewable buildout is beginning to show a similar price formation, with the curve dropping in the late morning hours. PJM's battery market is still emerging, and the grid has the smallest share of renewables. Its net load curve shows a more traditional shape with a steady increase throughout the day.

Key Insights

  1. CAISO represents the most mature storage, with arbitrage spreads beginning to shrink
  2. ERCOT follows a similar trajectory with growing deployment in a more volatile market
  3. PJM remains at an earlier stage with significant growth expected ahead
  4. Future of battery value evolves differently across the different markets

Battery Value Stream Evolution

x

The initial value for batteries is in their ability to respond quickly to frequency changes. High-power, short-duration batteries are well suited for this service, as reflected by ERCOT's 1.56-hour fleet average. With much lower marginal costs compared to gas alternatives and a relatively shallow market, prices quickly drop and ancillary services are often saturated first.

Energy arbitrage follows as a major source of revenue after, and is where battery duration becomes important, determining how much of the intraday price spread a battery can capture. Longer-duration assets like CAISO's 3.8-hour average offer greater flexibility with exposure to a wider range of pricing hours, and have more options to optimize returns.

However, growth in merchant energy trading eases the negative prices and high premiums over time, resulting in weaker revenues for stand-alone batteries. This is currently playing out in CAISO where negative pricing hours have decreased by 20% since 2024. As battery penetration grows, value streams evolve and more diverse revenue strategies are needed. The remaining opportunity beyond this point depends heavily on market design and volatility.

Market Design and Battery Opportunities

Hourly price variability (std dev $/MWh), Q3 2025.

CAISO

CAISO's market prices remained relatively stable in Q3 2025 due to mild summer conditions and a solar fleet that met up to 82% of daytime demand. With shrinking spreads, future battery value relies primarily on Resource Adequacy (RA) payments, providing increased revenue certainty but less upside than markets driven by volatility.

This dynamic favours co-located battery and solar projects. Developers benefit from a shared grid connection reduced solar curtailment, which increases the value of renewable generation. Because RA is typically procured by utilities and other load-serving entities, these become the long-term offtakers for battery projects in California.

ERCOT

ERCOT operates in an energy-only market, where reliability is secured through scarcity pricing rather than capacity payments. Unlike capacity markets, ERCOT only rewards resources for meeting real-time demand, providing no incentive for excess capacity to accumulate. As a result, scarcity pricing remains persistent and acts a transparent indicator of market needs, giving investors confidence that the value of the battery will endure as the market matures.

This means revenues continute to rely on scarcity events with the dominant contract structure in ERCOT being profit sharing, with higher upside potential. Market maturity sees a shift toward more downside protection from revenue uncertainty. Toll and floor agreements providing th underwriting from merchant operators/trading houses, owners keeping some upside.

PJM

PJM represents a hybrid case, operating in a capacity market that is experiencing increasing volatility. Capacity is more expensive to secure, clearing at the $329/MWh cap, combined with rapid demand growth, batteries in PJM are positioned to monetize both reliability and volatility. Capacity revenues provide stable contracted income, while exposure to market volatility provides merchant opportunities.

Capacity payments improve revenue certainty by compensating resources for being available, but as markets mature they can weaken scarcity signals and encourage excess build-out of capacity. For battery investors, this is pivotal because batteries earn from flexibility and spreads, rather than simple energy generation. The more a battery is committed for reliability, less freedom it has to respond to periods or extreme pricing.

PJM therefore sits between the two models. It offers revenue security while still benefitting from valuable merchant opportunities. Future development depends on whether scarcity signals remain strong enough to preserve battery value, and the developer's balance between reliability commitment and flexible operation of the asset.

Outlook

As ancillary service markets become saturated and energy arbitrage spreads compress, battery revenue stacks are defined by the underlying market design:

  1. CAISO: Monetizes reliability through Resource Adequacy payments, tolling arrangements and utility procurement.
  2. ERCOT: Monetizes volatility through scarcity pricing, profit-sharing structures and merchant exposure. Downside protection supported by floors or hybrid tolls.
  3. PJM: Monetizes both reliability and volatility through a combination of capacity revenues and merchant opportunities.
