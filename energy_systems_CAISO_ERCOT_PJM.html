The differences between PJM (little BESS penetration) and ERCOT (1-2 hour med penetration) and CAISO (4 hour heavy penetration) to give you an idea of the impact of the batteries on the grid. And the transition happens very quickly, so what does PJM look like if batteries become more proliferate?

Parameters that highlight structural differences in how these grids operate and the role batteries play in them.

1. Penetration & Deployment Parameters

These help you compare the scale and "density" of battery adoption.

  • Total Installed Capacity (MW/MWh): The basic measure of how much storage is available. CAISO and ERCOT currently lead the U.S. in these figures.
  • Penetration vs. Peak Load: Instead of raw numbers, look at capacity as a percentage of the system's peak demand (e.g., ~38% in CAISO vs. ~11% in ERCOT). This shows how "battery-heavy" the grid is relative to its size.
  • Average Duration (Hours): How long the "average" battery can discharge (e.g., 4-hour batteries in CAISO vs. 1-2 hour batteries in ERCOT). This tells you if the grid uses them for quick stabilization or longer-term energy shifting.

2. Operational Impact Parameters

What batteries are doing for the grid.

  • Daily Load Shifting (Energy Shifting): Measuring how much energy is moved from low-demand (or high-renewable) periods to peak evening periods. This is the most visible impact in "Duck Curve" markets like CAISO.
  • Renewable Curtailment Reduction: Tracking if higher BESS penetration leads to less wasted (curtailed) wind and solar power by storing it instead.
  • Ancillary Service Saturation: How much of the grid's "balancing" services (like frequency regulation) are now provided by batteries instead of traditional gas plants.

3. Price & Market Signal Parameters (The "Drivers")

Explain why BESS is being built.

  • Price Volatility (Price Spreads): Looking at the general gap between the daily highest and lowest prices. High volatility in ERCOT (due to wind fluctuations) drives BESS growth differently than the steadier patterns in CAISO.
  • Scarcity Events: The frequency of extreme price spikes. BESS impact is often highest during these "grid stress" events.
  • Capacity Market Participation: Whether batteries are getting paid just to "be there" (common in PJM) or must earn everything by actively trading (as in ERCOT).
